When Can You Cut Hedges on Farmland?

Hedges on farmland in England can't be cut or trimmed from 1 March to 31 August. So the farm hedge cutting season runs from 1 September to the end of February. Here's what the rules cover, the exceptions, and what it means for your hedges.

The short answer

Under the Management of Hedgerows (England) Regulations 2024, you can't cut or trim a farm hedge from 1 March to 31 August, inclusive, every year.

That leaves September to February. Most farm hedges are cut in the autumn and winter, after harvest and before birds start nesting again.

Which hedges the rules cover

The rules cover hedges growing on land used for agriculture that are at least 20 metres long, or shorter but joined to another hedge at each end.

Hedges round a house and its garden aren't covered by these rules.

When you can cut in the closed season

The law allows cutting between 1 March and 31 August when:

  • the hedge overhangs a road, highway or footpath with a right of way and blocks it or puts people in danger
  • the hedge blocks the view of road users or the light from a street lamp
  • the hedge is dead, diseased, damaged or badly rooted and likely to fall onto a road or path
  • it's for human or animal health and safety
  • it's to deal with a serious pest, weed or plant disease
  • you're sowing oilseed rape or temporary grassland in August, and you've told the Rural Payments Agency in writing first
  • you have written permission from the Rural Payments Agency

Hedge laying and coppicing

Hedge laying and coppicing can carry on until 30 April. A newly laid hedge can be trimmed by hand within six months of being laid.

The 2 metre rule

The same rules say you must keep green cover on the land within 2 metres of the centre of a farm hedge, and not cultivate it or put fertiliser or pesticide on it, apart from a few exceptions such as spot-treating certain weeds.

Garden hedges and nesting birds

Garden hedges aren't covered by the farm rules. But under the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981 it's an offence to damage or destroy a wild bird's nest while it's in use or being built. Nesting runs roughly from March to August, so we check before we cut.

When we cut

We cut farm, roadside and paddock hedges with a tractor-mounted flail from September to the end of February, round Silverstone, Towcester and Brackley. Send us a photo of your hedge on WhatsApp for a price.

Questions

Can I cut my farm hedge in August?

Only in a few cases, for example if it blocks a road or footpath, or if you're sowing oilseed rape or temporary grassland in August and have told the Rural Payments Agency in writing first.

When does hedge cutting season start?

1 September. Farm hedges can then be cut until the end of February.

Do the rules cover my garden hedge?

No. The farm hedge rules don't cover hedges round a house and garden, but it's still an offence to damage an active bird's nest.

Source: The Management of Hedgerows (England) Regulations 2024. Updated 17 November 2026.
